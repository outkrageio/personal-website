**File:** `docs/plans/2026-03-01-software-quality-age-of-ai-design.md`

**Meta section:**
- Title: Use the working title "Software Quality in the Age of AI" (or refine if a stronger manifesto-register title emerges)
- Description: One sentence for the blog listing — must convey the tension (opportunity AND risk), not just the reassurance
- Audience: Quality professionals — SDETs, QA engineers, test leads, anyone navigating AI's impact on their craft
- Tone: Manifesto energy. Practitioner authority. Paul Graham-level clarity with selective sharpness. NOT DHH-level heat throughout. Reference the internal voice models: `the-shepherd-and-the-sheep.mdx` for opening structure and first-person authority, `the-same-blind-spot-twice.mdx` for closing challenge structure. External calibration: Paul Graham's "Hackers and Painters" (directness) and "What You'll Wish You'd Known" (challenge framing).
- Length: 1500-2000 words (target 1700-1800)
- Approach: Two examples create tension resolved by quality systems thinking — examples first, synthesis after

**Core Thesis section:**
State the thesis as TENSION, not reassurance. Must contain both:
- The opportunity: AI makes quality engineers more powerful than ever (the FastAPI service proves it)
- The risk: This is only true if humans remain the arbiters of what quality means (the blind spot proves it)
- The resolution: Quality systems thinking — and it's hard work. "This is the work now, and it's yours."
Do NOT frame as "AI won't replace you." Frame as "the fear is wrong about the mechanism."

**Structure section — use DECLARATIVE CLAIM HEADERS, not topic labels:**
Each section header must pass the test: "Can a reader respond with agreement or disagreement?" If they can only respond with "okay, that's what this is," it's a topic label. Revise.

4-5 sections matching the build order. For each section, describe: what it does, key points, specific content, how it connects to the argument. Include word budget targets per section:

| Section | Target Words | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| Hook / reframe | 150-200 | Drops reader into the professional moment. Surfaces the fear as "the thing you've been thinking." Reframe states the thesis flatly. |
| Fear narrative acknowledgment | 100-150 | "You've been thinking it." Intimate, not confrontational. Surfaces the unspoken fear before the reframe. May be integrated into the hook rather than standalone — use judgment. |
| Force multiplier example | 300-350 | FastAPI diagnostic service. What it ingests (test results, logs, historical trends). What AI does (infers failure cause, proposes fix suggestions). Timing (one week vs. months for a traditional team). The emotional beat: where the reader feels the force multiplier impact. |
| Pivot sentence | 50-75 | ESCALATION, not reversal. The same force that makes AI a force multiplier is what creates the blind spot. "Yes, and this is exactly why" — not "but" or "however." |
| Blind spot example | 350-400 | Equal or greater length than force multiplier (required constraint). Composite/representative. Sketch 2-3 candidate domains: (1) payment edge case — refund calculation, rounding/currency, financial stakes; (2) data migration — records dropped or transformed incorrectly; (3) business rule validation — domain-agnostic, plausible-but-wrong rule interpretation. For each: the discovery moment (green tests, confident CI, fundamentally wrong business logic). Mark Phase 2's selection point explicitly. |
| Synthesis (quality systems thinking) | 200-250 | Named AFTER examples earn it. Three specific activities: designing quality gates, owning risk assessment, shifting left AND right. No hedging. |
| Close (challenge) | 150-200 | Professional directive. "This is the work now, and it's yours." Challenge, not comfort. Reader knows exactly what to do, and it feels hard. |

**Example sketches — more detailed than prior design docs:**

FastAPI force multiplier sketch:
- Lock key technical details: what it ingests (test results, logs, historical trends), what AI does with it (infers failure cause, proposes fix suggestions), build time (one week), traditional equivalent (months with a team)
- Lock emotional beat: where the reader feels the impact — an SDET with AI built a quality system that previously required a team and months of effort
- Lock 1-2 essential phrases that must survive into the final post (describe the rest by intent)

Blind spot example sketch:
- 2-3 candidate technical domains with brief sketches (see word budget table above)
- The discovery moment in enough detail that Phase 2 knows the exact emotional beat: green test suite, confident CI output, fundamentally wrong business logic discovered only when tests were written from acceptance criteria instead of code
- Mark Phase 2's selection point: "Phase 2 picks the strongest domain during drafting"
- Lock 1-2 essential phrases per example that must survive into the final post

**Argument arc (from CONTEXT.md locked decisions):**
- Pivot is ESCALATION: the success CAUSES the blind spot. Same force, different angle.
- After blind spot: personal recognition first ("I've done this"), then industry-wide alarm ("this is happening everywhere")
- Resolution: clarity with weight. Reader knows exactly what to do, and it feels hard.
- Fear narrative: "You've been thinking it." Intimate, not confrontational. Trust-building before reframe.

**Voice and register constraints (encode explicitly in the design doc):**
- No hedging language ("might," "could," "perhaps" in claims — allowed in examples)
- No passive voice on claims ("AI has been shown to..." — forbidden)
- No tool names (not FastAPI's marketing name, not any vendor)
- No "adapt or die" urgency rhetoric
- No em dashes (project-wide style preference)
- Italics for emphasis on key words allowed
- Author speaks as practitioner: "I've built these systems. I've seen this fail."
- Controlled heat: measured authority with deliberate sharp moments
- Strategic first-person: "I" for examples and key claims, "you" and "we" for synthesis and challenge

**Key Risks section:**
Identify what could go wrong in execution. At minimum:
- Thesis slipping into reassurance ("still valuable" as conclusion rather than earned argument)
- Pivot reading as reversal instead of escalation
- Example sketches being too abstract for Phase 2 to execute against
- Fear narrative resolving too quickly (before examples earn the reframe)

Do NOT write any prose for the blog post itself. This document is structural only.
