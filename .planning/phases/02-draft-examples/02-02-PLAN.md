**PART A: Pivot sentence (EXMP-03) -- 50-75 words, 1-3 sentences**

Insert the pivot immediately after the blind spot example section. This is structural weight, not length.

**Critical constraint: ESCALATION, not reversal.**

The pivot connects the two examples by showing they share the same mechanism: the same force that built the diagnostic service in one week is the same force that produced a green suite validating wrong behavior. Velocity without human quality gates is the mechanism. Same force. Different angle.

Required logic pattern:
"The same force that [did X] is the same force that [did Y].
[The mechanism] is the same. [The stakes] are different."

Forbidden connectives (these read as reversal):
- "but"
- "however"
- "on the other hand"
- "yet"
- "although"
- "unfortunately"

Required logic: "yes, and this is exactly why." The force multiplier EXPLAINS the blind spot. The success CAUSED the risk. The pivot should feel inevitable, not surprising. The reader should think: "of course."

Test the pivot after writing: read it aloud. Does it say "the same force caused both"? Or does it say "but this is the problem"? If the latter, rewrite.

**PART B: Fear narrative placeholder (ARG-02) -- 100-150 words**

Add the fear narrative as a clearly labeled placeholder section at the BOTTOM of the file, after all other content. This will be moved to its final position (before the force multiplier, integrated into the hook) during Phase 4.

- Name the fear honestly: AI is coming for QA jobs. The reader has been thinking it.
- Use intimate register, not confrontational: "You've been thinking it." -- reader feels seen, not attacked.

- NOT include the reframe. The reframe belongs in the hook (Phase 4) when the destination is known.

**Voice constraints (same as all Phase 2 content):**
- No tool names, no vendor names, no em dashes
- No hedging on the acknowledgment: the fear is real, stated plainly
- No passive voice
- No "adapt or die" urgency rhetoric
