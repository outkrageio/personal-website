**Reference:** Design doc section "AI Gave One Engineer the Power of a Team"

**Required technical details to render (from design doc, locked):**
- A diagnostic service for quality analysis
- What it ingests: test results (pass/fail history across builds), runtime logs, historical failure trends
- What AI does with the inputs: infers failure cause (pattern matching across all three inputs), proposes fix suggestions ranked by confidence
- Build time: one week, solo
- Traditional equivalent: months with a dedicated team and dedicated infrastructure

**Essential phrases that MUST survive into the prose:**
- "The capability threshold shifted." (or close equivalent where "threshold shifted" lands)
- The contrast: "one week" vs "months with a team" stated plainly, not dressed up

**Emotional beat to land:** The moment is not "wow, that was fast." The moment is the realization that the capability threshold moved. Things that required a team are now within reach of one engineer who knows how to direct AI. The reader should feel: "I could do that. And that changes what my role is."

**Voice and register constraints (checkable rules):**
- Author speaks as practitioner: "I've built this. I watched it happen." Authority from direct experience, not theory.
- First-person for examples and key claims ("I" voice)
- Show the impact through specifics, do NOT announce it. Do not write "this was impressive" or "this was remarkable." Give the reader the details and let them feel the weight.
- No tool names. Not the framework name, not the AI provider, not any vendor. The pattern is the point.
- No em dashes anywhere. Use periods or commas instead.
- No hedging on claims: no "might," "could," "perhaps" on thesis-level statements
- No passive voice on key claims: "AI does..." not "AI has been shown to..."
- Controlled heat: measured authority with deliberate sharp moments. Mostly calm.
- Italics for emphasis allowed but selective, not decorative

**Voice models to calibrate against (read both posts first):**
- "The Shepherd and the Sheep": "I've watched it happen. I've done it." -- personal, unambiguous, no announcement
- "The Shepherd and the Sheep": "Give a senior engineer Claude Code and clear autonomy, and they will build in a week what used to take a team a quarter. That's not hyperbole." -- specific claim, plainly stated (NOTE: the blog post names tools, but THIS post must not. Use this as a model for the rhythm and confidence of the claim, not the specificity of tool names.)

**Anti-patterns to avoid:**
- "This was remarkable" or "That is the astonishing thing" -- never tell the reader how to feel
- Passive voice on the key claim: "A service was built that could..." -- use active: "One engineer built a system that..."
- Announcing emotion instead of causing it
- Using any specific technology brand names
