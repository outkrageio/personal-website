# Project Retrospective

*A living document updated after each milestone. Lessons feed forward into future planning.*

## Milestone: v1.0 — Software Quality in the Age of AI

**Shipped:** 2026-03-01
**Phases:** 5 | **Plans:** 6 | **Sessions:** ~3

### What Was Built
- 1,702-word manifesto-style blog post at /blog/software-quality-age-of-ai
- Design doc with declarative claim headers and ESCALATION pivot structure
- Implementation plan with examples-first build order
- Full GSD workflow from research through publication

### What Worked
- Examples-first build order: writing anchor examples before hook and close meant the opening and closing were written with full knowledge of the destination — no thesis-first trap
- Declarative claim headers in the design doc kept every phase aligned without re-reading prior prose
- ESCALATION pivot (not reversal): "yes, and" logic prevented bait-and-switch experience between force multiplier and blind spot examples
- Two-beat discovery moment in blind spot: personal recognition before industry alarm earned the right to make the broader claim
- Phase-level atomic commits: each plan produced a verifiable artifact that the next phase consumed

### What Was Inefficient
- Word count undershot on first full assembly (1,491 vs 1,500 minimum): Phase 5 needed to expand synthesis and close by ~260 words — could have been avoided by building in word count margin during Phases 3-4
- SUMMARY frontmatter in Phase 1 left `requirements-completed` empty — a minor documentation gap caught in audit
- ROADMAP.md checkbox for 05-01-PLAN.md left unchecked despite phase being complete — a documentation hygiene issue

### Patterns Established
- Earned naming: concept names (like "quality systems thinking") arrive after examples prove them, not before
- Voice constraints as explicit rules: checkable forbidden/required lists survive across phases better than implicit tone guidance
- Placeholder sections with JSX comments: label deferred content with explicit Phase N attribution
- Expansion-only revision: never touch calibrated sections (hook, examples); only expand synthesis and close

### Key Lessons
1. Design docs with declarative headers are more useful than topic-label outlines — they force argument structure decisions upfront
2. Writing the hook last (after examples and synthesis) produces a tighter opening because the destination is known
3. Word count margins should be built into intermediate phases — relying on a final revision pass to fix undercount adds unnecessary risk
4. The GSD workflow (research → plan → execute → verify) maps well to creative writing, not just code — phases produce verifiable artifacts at each step

### Cost Observations
- Model mix: 100% opus (all planning and execution)
- Sessions: ~3 (init/planning, phases 1-3, phases 4-5 + audit)
- Notable: 23 minutes total execution across 6 plans — extremely fast for a 1,700-word post with full structural planning
