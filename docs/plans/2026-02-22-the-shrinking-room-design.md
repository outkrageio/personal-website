# Blog Post Design: The Shrinking Room

## Meta

- **Title:** The Shrinking Room
- **Description:** Engineering teams are getting smaller and output is going up. What determines who stays in the room?
- **Audience:** IC engineers thinking about their careers in the age of AI
- **Tone:** Quieter, more reflective than previous posts. Honest uncertainty. Personal and opinionated but with more "I don't know" than "here's what's true."
- **Length:** ~3,000-4,000 words
- **Approach:** Observe the shift, explore who thrives and who struggles, confront the ideas bottleneck, land with a balanced path forward

## Structure

### Opening Hook

Concrete observation, not a prediction. Teams shipping the most are getting smaller. Not layoffs or cost-cutting, but a few engineers with the right tools and instincts doing what used to require a department. Quiet, factual, unsettling implications.

### Section: "The Math Is Changing"

Factual setup. Teams shrinking through attrition not backfilled, projects that needed five done by two, startups launching with tiny headcounts. The ratio of engineers to output is shifting fast. Honest uncertainty about where it lands. Not claiming mass unemployment, not claiming demand absorbs surplus. The number is lower than before and nobody knows the equilibrium.

### Section: "What Gets You a Seat"

Three overlapping traits that determine who stays:

- **Scope of thinking** — System/product level vs. function level. Function-level thinkers get replaced by agents. System-level thinkers direct them.
- **Adaptability** — Who learns the new way fast enough. Seniority isn't protection. Willingness to change is. Some juniors leapfrog seniors.
- **Taste and judgment** — When agents produce ten solutions in the time it took to produce one, knowing which is good becomes the bottleneck. Hard to teach, takes experience, about to become the most valuable skill.

Not separate filters but a cluster.

### Section: "The Executor Problem"

Uncomfortable truth with balance. Most engineers have been executors — receive spec, build spec, ship spec. Industry hired for this, interviewed for this, rewarded this. Execution is exactly what AI collapses.

Path forward: execution transforms into evaluation. The executor becomes the evaluator. Skill shifts from typing to judgment about what correct looks like. Some make this transition. Some won't. Unknown ratio.

### Section: "When Ideas Become the Bottleneck"

When building is cheap, what do you build? Engineering operated with execution as the constraint. Backlogs were infinite, limiting factor was hands on keyboards. When that limit lifts, you need ideas. Generating good ideas is a different skill than building them. Most orgs don't have it in abundance.

Two possibilities: demand expands (build things never justified before — internal tools, experiments, niche products) and engineers stay employed differently. Or it doesn't, and surplus capacity becomes surplus headcount. Genuine uncertainty.

### Section: "The Path Nobody Talks About"

Balanced landing. What engineers can actually do: think at higher abstraction, direct agents instead of being one, develop product opinions, build evaluation skills. These are learnable, not personality traits.

Honest about limits: individual skill doesn't change macro picture. If industry needs fewer engineers, personal development determines which side of the shift you're on, not whether the shift happens.

### Closing

Short. The room is getting smaller. Don't know how small. The people who will be in it are distinguishing themselves now — not by writing more code, but by knowing what's worth building and whether it was built right.
